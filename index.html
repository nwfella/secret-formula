How to play

The goal

Each equation is missing its coefficients — the numbers in front of the compounds. Change them until every element has the same number of atoms on both sides. That is what "balanced" means in chemistry: mass is conserved, so atoms are never created or destroyed.

Only the numbers in the dark boxes change. The formulas themselves are fixed — you cannot add atoms to a compound, only count how many of each compound take part.

Reading the ledger

The table under the equation counts every element on the left and on the right, multiplied by the coefficients you have set. The Δ column is right − left. When every row reads 0 and every Δ is green, the equation is balanced.

Winning

An equation is solved the moment it balances — there is no confirm button. If you balance it with multiples of the smallest numbers (4 H₂ + 2 O₂ → 4 H₂O instead of 2 H₂ + O₂ → 2 H₂O) the game still accepts it and tells you, because chemists write the smallest whole numbers by convention.

If you get stuck

Hint works in three steps: a general method, then the specific element that is furthest out, then one exact coefficient. Hints are never counted against you, and Undo steps backwards one change at a time. Start over returns every coefficient to 1.

What is real here

All 48 equations are real reactions with real products. Every one has exactly one valid ratio — that is checked at build time by the same solver the page runs. Molar masses are computed from standard atomic weights. Nothing is a guess and nothing is a placeholder.
